Three things to keep in mind when you read this table:

- **An "application" is not the same unit everywhere.** Some services find the job, tailor the resume and submit the form. Others charge extra for parts of that, or count a software submission you never see. Compare what is included, not only the count.
- **These are vendor statements.** Each figure is what the provider publishes about itself. We have not tested these services, and a price says nothing about the quality of the work.
- **Prices move.** ApplyAll's and LazyApply's figures are marked-down prices, and any of them can change after the date above. Each page shows dollar signs but does not say which country version we saw. Confirm on the live page before you buy.

## How we built this list

We looked for services with a price you can read without talking to sales, and we used one rule: a figure goes in only if we read it on the provider's own page on November 5, 2026. We did not use review sites, and we did not fill gaps from memory.

That rule kept some well-known names out of the table. AIApply describes its plans and Auto-Apply credit packs on its homepage without a dollar figure, and its pricing address sends you back to that homepage. Sonara's pricing page did not load for us. Simplify's pricing address returned a not-found page. LoopCV's page shows only a starting price in euros, not its plans. The list is a sample of what is public, not a full market survey, and leaving a service out says nothing about its quality.

We compared each service on the things you can check before you pay: who does the work, what you can control, what proof you get, how you reach a person, and how you pay.

## Which kind of service do you need?

Most of the confusion in this market comes from comparing three different purchases as if they were one.

**Human application services.** A person finds or receives the jobs and completes the forms. ApplyAll, Careerify, Scale.jobs and Scout's Human Assistant plans sit here. You are buying labor, so you should expect someone to handle exceptions and answer you.

**Automation tools.** Software submits or fills applications from your saved information. ApplyPass, JobCopilot and LazyApply sit here, and Scout's AI Assistant uses automated fulfillment too, inside a managed service. This route usually costs less, and it asks more of you, because you need to set clear rules and check the first results.

**Reverse recruiters.** These services add positioning, outreach to hiring managers and coaching on top of applications. Career Agents, Find My Profession and My Personal Recruiter sit here. The fee is higher because the scope is wider. Read [what a reverse recruiter costs](/blog/reverse-recruiter-cost) if this is the route you are weighing.

## What each service publishes

Listed alphabetically again. Each entry reports what the provider's own page says, and nothing here is a test result.

### ApplyAll

[ApplyAll's homepage](https://www.applyall.com/) (read November 5, 2026) sells one payment rather than a subscription: 100 hand-reviewed applications for $249 or 200 for $299, both shown as sale prices against $299 and $349. It says real people verify that each job matches your criteria and fill out the forms, that it aims for 10 to 20 applications a day, and that it skips applications with open-ended questions it cannot answer from your resume.

Its Interview Guarantee refunds 100% of your payment if you get no qualifying interview within 30 days of application completion. Read the definition: it must be a live first-round phone or video interview with someone who works for the hiring company. Third-party recruiters, technical assessments, take-home exercises and automated screeners do not count. See also [Scout compared with ApplyAll](/compare/applyall-alternative).

### ApplyPass

[ApplyPass's pricing page](https://www.applypass.com/pricing) (read November 5, 2026) lists a free Basic plan with 7 applications a week, Momentum at $99 a month for 100 a week, and Premium at $199 a month for 400 a week. Platinum, a coaching program, is "Contact Us". The page is titled "Automate Your Job Search" but does not explain how applications are sent, so ask before you buy. Cheaper plans show older listings: 7 days old on Basic and 3 days old on Momentum.

### Career Agents

[Career Agents' reverse recruiting page](https://careeragents.org/reverse-recruitment/) (read November 5, 2026) lists Pro at $999 a month for 7 months ($6,993) and Executive at $1,667 a month for 9 months, with a listed package price of $14,999. Both list 5 to 10 applications a day from a dedicated Career Agent, plus outreach, interview preparation and salary negotiation support. Its $999 Starter package states "No Applications Included". Pro and Executive carry a conditional offer guarantee of 50% back or a free extension. The page gives the extension as both 4 and 6 months, so get the term in writing.

### Careerify

[Careerify's pricing page](https://www.careerify.io/pricing) (read November 5, 2026) sells recruiter hours: $150 a month for 10 hours and about 50 applications, $280 for 20 hours and about 100, $455 for 35 hours and about 175, and $720 for 60 hours and about 300. Quarterly billing saves 15%.

Its [apply-to-jobs guide](https://careerify.io/apply-to-jobs-for-me) (page marked "Updated 28 October 2026", read November 5, 2026) says the counts are estimates and that "the guaranteed number is lower and in writing." It also says recruiters are offshore and work mostly on weekdays. Every plan starts with a 5-day trial at $0 today, card required, and an Application Delivery Guarantee gives you a month free if the guaranteed number is not met.

### Find My Profession

[Find My Profession's reverse recruiter page](https://www.findmyprofession.com/reverse-recruiter/) (read November 5, 2026) offers two options: $3,000 a month with no success fee, or $1,500 a month plus an 8% success fee on first-year salary. Services renew every 30 days. For scale, 8% of a $100,000 salary is $8,000. That salary is our example, not theirs.

### JobCopilot

[JobCopilot's pricing page](https://jobcopilot.com/pricing/) (read November 5, 2026) quotes daily prices: Premium "starts from" $0.93 a day with 1 copilot and up to 20 job matches daily, and Elite from $1.05 a day with 3 copilots and up to 50. It says weekly, monthly and quarterly plans exist but does not list them separately, so check the total you will be charged for the billing period you pick. Both plans include automated applications, a tracker, resume and cover letter builders, and mock interviews.

### LazyApply

[LazyApply's homepage](https://lazyapply.com/) (read November 5, 2026) lists yearly plans: $99 for 15 applications a day and 1 resume profile, $149 for 150 a day and 5 profiles, and $999 for 1,500 a day and 20 profiles, each shown against a higher price. It names Greenhouse, Dice, Indeed and ZipRecruiter and offers a 30-day refund policy. The page says profiles "will never get blocked by platforms". We cannot verify that, so read each platform's own rules. See [Scout compared with LazyApply](/compare/lazyapply-alternative).

### My Personal Recruiter

[My Personal Recruiter's homepage FAQ](https://mypersonalrecruiter.com/) (read November 5, 2026) lists Basic at $899 a month, Standard at $1,499 and Premium at $2,499, each with a dedicated recruiter, resume and LinkedIn strategy, job applications and interview support. It says most clients stay 2 to 4 months and that most of its clients earn $100,000 or more. Its [pricing page](https://mypersonalrecruiter.com/pricing/) says that if you do not secure an offer within six months of active participation, it continues at no extra charge. Its own [blog post](https://mypersonalrecruiter.com/can-you-hire-someone-to-find-you-a-job/) quotes $2,000 to $5,000 a month for reverse recruiters generally, so ask which tier matches the work you expect.

### Scale.jobs

Scale.jobs sells both ways, and its site is not consistent about that. Its [one-time page](https://scale.jobs/pricing/one-time) (read November 5, 2026) says "We don't do subscriptions" and lists $399 for 500 applications, $599 for 1,000 and $999 for 1,500. Its [monthly page](https://scale.jobs/pricing/subscription) (read November 5, 2026) lists $99 for 100, $149 for 250, $199 for 500 and $499 for 750.

The two are not the same bundle. One-time plans include job finding, AI custom resumes and cover letters. On the monthly plans below $499, We Find Jobs and AI Custom Resumes are optional add-ons at $49, $79 or $99 each depending on the plan, and the page does not say whether they bill once or every month. Its [pricing page](https://scale.jobs/pricing) promises a proof screenshot for every application. Its [cancellation policy](https://scale.jobs/cancellation-policy) (read November 5, 2026) sets the refund at plan price minus applications sent minus a one-time $100 fee. For the long version, see [Scout compared with Scale.jobs](/compare/scale-jobs-alternative).

### Scout

We make this one, so weigh this entry accordingly. Scout has two lanes in one account. The AI Assistant has two plans: AI Essential at $39 a month for 75 applications and 1 active job profile, and AI Plus at $79 for 200 applications and up to 3 profiles. The Human Assistant has three plans: Focused Search at $299 for 250 applications and 1 profile, Full Search at $499 for 500 and up to 2 profiles, and Career Campaign at $999 for 1,000, 2 Human Assistants and up to 4 profiles.

Human plans include a dedicated WhatsApp group and screenshots of important form answers. AI plans do not include form-answer screenshots. Both lanes show the job, its status and the resume used. There is no placement fee and no percentage of salary, and there is no interview or offer guarantee.

## When another service is better

A list written by one of its entrants should say where that entrant loses. Here is where we think Scout is not the best fit. Outside figures in this section come from the linked pages, read November 5, 2026.

- **You want the lowest published price for human-completed applications.** Choose [Scale.jobs](https://scale.jobs/pricing/one-time). Its one-time plans cost about $0.60 to $0.80 per application by our arithmetic, with job finding included, against about $1.00 to $1.20 on Scout's Human plans. On the published numbers, Scale.jobs is cheaper.
- **You want a proof screenshot of every application.** [Scale.jobs](https://scale.jobs/pricing) says it provides one. Scout's Human plans cover important form answers, and Scout's AI plans do not include form-answer screenshots.
- **You want money back tied to an interview.** [ApplyAll](https://www.applyall.com/) publishes a conditional refund. Scout has no interview or offer guarantee. Employers decide who they interview, and we do not sell a promise we cannot control.
- **You want to try the work before you pay.** [Careerify](https://www.careerify.io/pricing) publishes a 5-day trial with real recruiter hours, card required.
- **You want to pay once and be done.** [ApplyAll](https://www.applyall.com/) and [Scale.jobs](https://scale.jobs/pricing/one-time) sell one-time packages. Scout plans are subscriptions, or 90 days prepaid.
- **Your bottleneck is not the application.** If you need outreach to hiring managers, interview coaching or negotiation help, a reverse recruiter covers work that Scout's plans do not. [My Personal Recruiter](https://mypersonalrecruiter.com/), [Find My Profession](https://www.findmyprofession.com/reverse-recruiter/) and [Career Agents](https://careeragents.org/reverse-recruitment/) all sell that wider scope. Check for salary fees: [Reverse Recruiting Agency](https://www.reverserecruitingagency.com/pricing), for example, lists $1,500 a month plus 10% of first-year salary.
- **Budget is the main limit and you will supervise software.** [LazyApply](https://lazyapply.com/) starts at $99 a year and [JobCopilot](https://jobcopilot.com/pricing/) from $0.93 a day, and [ApplyPass](https://www.applypass.com/pricing) has a free plan.
- **You have the time and only want faster forms.** [Simplify Copilot](https://simplify.jobs/copilot) says its autofill, tracker and job matches are free, with no limit on applications. You still apply yourself, but you pay nothing for those features.

## What to check before you pay any service

These checks apply to every entry above, including ours.

1. **What counts as an application?** Ask whether finding the job, tailoring the resume and writing the cover letter are in the price or sold separately.
2. **What proof will you get?** Ask for a sample record before you pay. Our [proof checklist](/blog/what-proof-should-job-application-assistant-provide) shows what to look for.
3. **What happens to unused volume?** Get refund, cancellation and rollover terms in writing, including any fixed fee.
4. **What are the conditions on any guarantee?** Read what counts as an interview, when the clock starts, and what you must do to stay eligible.
5. **Is there a salary fee?** Some reverse recruiters add a percentage of first-year salary to the monthly fee. Work it out in dollars before you sign.
6. **Does the method break a platform's rules?** LinkedIn says it does not allow third-party software or browser extensions that automate activity on its website ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1340567), read November 5, 2026). Read [whether auto-apply can get your account restricted](/blog/can-auto-apply-get-your-job-board-account-restricted) and [how Scout approaches application safety](/safety).
7. **Are you paying for work or for a promise of a job?** The FTC's [job scams page](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/job-scams) (read November 5, 2026) warns that honest placement firms do not typically charge candidates, and says to walk away if a placement firm asks for a fee, especially in advance. Paying for application work you can see is a different purchase from paying someone who claims they can place you. If a seller promises the job itself, stop. See [are job application services legit](/blog/are-job-application-services-legit).

Then start small. Whatever you pick, review the first batch line by line: right titles, right locations, truthful answers, correct resume. A first batch tells you more than a sales page does, and it protects you from buying the largest package before you know the work is good. If you are still deciding whether to buy at all, read [is a job application service worth it](/blog/is-a-job-application-service-worth-it).

If price per application is your main test, Scale.jobs' published numbers beat ours. If the search itself is the problem, a reverse recruiter may be worth the higher fee.
