## What does the price cover, and what does it not?

- **Covered:** finding suitable openings, preparing your materials, completing the applications, and a record of each job, its status and the resume used.
- **Not charged:** a placement fee.
- **Not promised:** interviews or offers. Employers decide who they interview and hire. If a service guarantees a job, treat that as a warning sign. Read [what to do when a service produces no interviews](/blog/job-application-service-no-interviews).
- **What you still do:** set your target roles, locations and dealbreakers, and check the first results.

Reverse recruiting is a wider purchase than application work. A reverse recruiter usually adds positioning, networking, outreach to hiring managers and coaching, so the monthly fee is higher.

## What drives the price of a job application service?

1. **Who does the work.** Software costs less than a dedicated person. A person handles exceptions, questions and communication, which is why Human Assistant plans cost more than AI plans.
2. **How many applications you get.** Allowances range from 75 to 1,000 a month across Scout's five plans.
3. **How much evidence you receive.** A record of every job, status and resume used is the baseline. <HumanPlanFeatures variant="price-driver" />
4. **How wide the scope is.** Form-filling is one purchase. Outreach, coaching and negotiation support are another.
5. **How you pay.** Prepaying 90 days lowers the price by about 10% compared with paying monthly.

## How do you compare cost fairly?

A low monthly price and a low price per application are not the same thing. Use this formula:

**True cost per qualified application = (service fee + add-ons + value of your time) ÷ verified applications that met your rules**

Then check five things for every provider: what is included, how many applications fit your rules, what proof you get, what you must correct, and what happens to unused volume. Read [what proof an application assistant should give you](/blog/what-proof-should-job-application-assistant-provide) for a checklist. A smaller first batch teaches you more than a polished sales page, so do not buy the largest package until the first results pass your review.

## Is a job application service worth the cost? A break-even example

Worth depends on your time, and the arithmetic is simple. This example uses one assumption, which is not a statistic: that completing one application yourself takes you 10 minutes.

- **AI Essential:** 75 applications × 10 minutes = 12.5 hours. At $39 a month, the plan breaks even if your time is worth more than about $3.12 an hour.
- **Focused Search:** 250 applications × 10 minutes = about 41.7 hours. At $299 a month, it breaks even if your time is worth more than about $7.18 an hour.
- **Full Search:** 500 applications × 10 minutes = about 83.3 hours. At $499 a month, it breaks even above about $5.99 an hour.

Swap in your own minutes per application. The calculation ignores your review time, wrong-fit applications and corrections, and it says nothing about interviews or offers. It only tells you whether the time you would spend is worth more than the price. If your real problem is an unclear target role or a resume that is not working, more applications will not fix it; fix those first.

## What should you avoid?

- **A guaranteed job or interview.** Employers decide, and no provider controls that. Be careful about paying anyone for the promise of a job itself, as opposed to paying for application work you can see.
- **Unlimited claims with no detail.** Ask about daily limits, supported sites and fair-use rules.
- **No record of what was sent.** If a service cannot show the job, status and resume used, you cannot check the work.
- **Unclear refund and cancellation terms.** Ask for them in writing before you pay.
- **Automation that breaks a platform's rules.** LinkedIn's help page on automated activity says it does not allow third-party software or browser extensions that automate activity on its website, and that this violates its User Agreement ([LinkedIn Help: Automated activity on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1340567), fetched November 1, 2026). A service should explain where and how it operates. Read [application safety](/safety) for how Scout approaches it.
