# Scout blog plan — Search Console update

**Prepared:** August 21, 2026

**Data source:** `applyscout.app-Performance-on-Search-2026-08-21`

**Search Console filter:** Web search, last three months

**Daily data available:** July 16–August 18, 2026
**Purpose:** Turn the broader SEO strategy into a performance-led blog queue without overreacting to a small, young dataset.

## Executive decision

The current plan has the right commercial and trust themes, but it needs four improvements:

1. Separate **new-content decisions** from **existing-page optimization**. Several current posts already rank near page one or two and should be strengthened rather than replaced by overlapping articles.
2. Organize publishing around **intent clusters and internal links**, not a flat keyword list.
3. Treat exact Search Console evidence as directional until each page has enough impressions. The export contains only 497 total impressions and 16 clicks.
4. Add a repeatable measurement loop with query groups, conversion events, and thresholds for refreshing, consolidating, or expanding a topic.

The next five posts therefore support the two clusters already showing the best early positions—service selection and application safety—while the existing Workday page is refreshed for the secondary query “Workday application help.” New Greenhouse or Lever blog posts are deferred because Scout already has landing pages targeting those exact terms; publishing duplicate-intent articles could split relevance.

## Editorial standard for every new post

Every article should include:

1. A direct 40–70 word answer before the first heading.
2. One primary search intent and a documented boundary from adjacent pages.
3. A decision table, process, checklist, or calculation that adds information beyond a summary.
4. Clear limitations; no promise of interviews, offers, universal safety, or platform coverage.
5. Links to the relevant hub, one or two sibling articles, and the most appropriate product path.
6. Exact source links and a checked date for prices, platform rules, or other changing claims.
7. A unique cover and useful explanatory visuals whose important information is also present in text.
8. Accurate frontmatter, descriptive alt text, and a visible update date after material changes.

The reader should be able to act on the article without buying Scout. The Scout CTA should follow from the decision rather than interrupt it.

## Measurement plan

Record the publication date, target query group, page type, internal links, and primary conversion for every URL.

Measure at 7, 14, and 28 days:

- Indexed status.
- Non-brand impressions and clicks.
- Number of distinct relevant queries.
- Queries and pages in positions 1–10, 11–20, and 21–50.
- CTR by position band, not only sitewide CTR.
- Pricing-page clicks.
- AI registrations.
- Human strategy-call clicks and bookings.
- Assisted conversions where the blog was an earlier landing page.

Use these initial action thresholds:

- **Position 8–20, at least 30 impressions, CTR below 2%:** test title and description while preserving intent.
- **Position 21–50 with relevant impressions:** improve answer depth, internal links, and evidence before changing the target.
- **Two Scout URLs repeatedly shown for the same intent:** choose a primary URL, differentiate or consolidate the other, and update internal links.
- **Indexed but no relevant impressions after 28–42 days:** review intent, crawl paths, and SERP fit; do not add length automatically.
- **Traffic without qualified product actions:** reassess CTA and funnel alignment rather than celebrating clicks alone.

## Decision log

- **Do not create Greenhouse and Lever blog copies this week.** Existing `/auto-apply/` landing pages already own those exact targets.
- **Do not localize the blog from country totals alone.** Query-page-country intersections and conversions are missing.
- **Do not optimize for generic “Scout” traffic.** Most visible variants are ambiguous or unrelated; strengthen the complete brand descriptor.
- **Do not use average position as a standalone KPI.** Query mix and tiny samples make it unstable.
- **Do not measure application count as the only product proof.** Qualified applications, wrong-fit rate, duplicates prevented, corrections, evidence, and interviews per qualified application are more useful.
